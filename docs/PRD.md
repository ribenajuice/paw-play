# PRD: Paw Play

*Owned by the product-manager agent. Written during `/kickoff`, updated by `/feature`. Keep under 2 pages.*

## Problem
Parents of toddlers who want screen time to be calm, safe, and self-contained have few options that aren't riddled with ads, surprise purchase prompts, or timers/fail states that frustrate a 2-5 year old. The founder wants a single app their son can open on his own — including with the phone handed over and the parent not watching — that offers a small, growing shelf of simple games, with zero risk of him tapping into a purchase, an ad, or an external link from any of them.

## Users
- **Primary**: the founder's son, age 2-5. Limited or no reading. Taps and drags, not menus. Gets frustrated by failure states, timers, or anything that punishes exploration.
- **Secondary**: the founder, as the one who hands over the phone and needs zero-oversight confidence that nothing bad can happen, in any game, ever.

## The one thing
A toddler can open the app to a picture-only home screen, tap a game tile, play that game to a satisfying completion, and get back to the home screen to pick another — with no reading required anywhere and no way to reach anything money-related, an ad, or an external link, from the home screen or from inside any individual game.

## Product shape
This is a **hub app**: one home screen showing a shelf of game tiles (icons only, no labels needed), each tile launching a self-contained mini-game. We are not building every game up front — Milestone 1 ships the hub with exactly **one** working game (the matching game originally scoped, now the first tile). Each future `/feature` adds one more game to the shelf; the hub itself doesn't change shape to do that. See `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` for how a new game gets added without touching the others.

**Long-term target: roughly a dozen small games on the shelf**, built up one at a time over many `/feature` runs, not a fixed roadmap set now. The home screen's tile grid is built to reflow into more columns as tiles are added (see `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md`'s `AdaptiveSquareGrid`), so this was accounted for from the shelf's first version rather than something to rework later at tile #5 or #10.

**Out of scope for Milestone 2 (v2):** a manual undo or restart button (auto-undo covers it), hints, per-tube themes, saved progress, more than 9 tubes, and any sound/music setting.

### Milestone 3 — Third game on the shelf: Paw Kitchen

Working name **Paw Kitchen**, chosen by the team; renaming is a content-only change. Like Paw Pour, it must be a pure addition per `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md`: a new game package plus one `GameCatalog` line, no change to Paw Match, Paw Pour or the hub shell.*

Vocabulary: an **order** is a dish plus the set of ingredients in its bubble; the **dish** is what the child is building on the counter; the **tray** is the row/grid of ingredients to tap.

**Menu (three dishes, each with a 6-ingredient shelf; the base is always in the order).** Burger: bun, patty, cheese, tomato, lettuce, pickle. Pizza: dough, sauce, cheese, mushroom, olive, pepperoni. Ice cream: cone, strawberry scoop, chocolate scoop, cherry, sprinkles, wafer. An ingredient appears at most once per order; there are no quantities to count. **The founder confirmed this starting menu (2026-09-24) and asked that more dishes can be added later:** a dish is defined as content (a base, a shelf of up to 6 ingredients, and their pictures), so adding pancakes, sandwiches, soup or cupcakes later needs no change to how orders, the tray, serving or the ramp work, and never touches the other games.

### Milestone 5 — Fifth game on the shelf: Paw Blocks
*A simplified block-placing puzzle in the spirit of Tetris / Block Blast: fill a whole row or column and it clears. The founder knows similar games exist and wants this one simpler and friendlier for a 4-year-old: **nothing falls, there is no speed**; he is offered 3 blocks at a time and places them wherever he likes. "Paw Blocks" is a working name chosen by the team (game `id` `paw-blocks`); renaming is a content-only change. Like the earlier games it is a pure addition per `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md`: a new package plus one `GameCatalog` line, no change to any other game or the hub shell.*

**Problem it solves:** a 2-5 year old (here, a 4-year-old) enjoys "fit the piece, make a line, watch it vanish" puzzles, but the popular versions show score, combos and "game over" when the board fills, and some are ad-funded. This one is a calm, open-ended play loop with no scoring and nothing to lose. **Founder decisions (settled; the last three confirmed by the founder on 2026-09-24):** (1) when the board is too full, the game gently clears space and play carries on, and it tries to prevent that in the first place; (2) blocks are dragged from a tray to the board, snapping to a grid, with a ghost outline; an illegal drop returns the block softly; (3) small board and simple blocks first, growing as he clears lines, session-only, no numbers; (4) the board may grow all the way to **9x9**; (5) the clear-out is a quiet sparkle, with no animal tidying up; (6) the board starts empty.

Vocabulary: the **board** is the square grid of cells; a **block** is a small shape of 1-4 cells; the **tray** is the row of 3 blocks on offer; a **line clear** is a completely filled row or column disappearing.

**Ramp** (a "clear" = one placement that completes at least one line; a double line still counts as one; clear-outs do not count; dots stay in every stage as an easy fallback):

| Stage | Advances after | Board | Blocks on offer (bars come both horizontal and vertical) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | start | 5x5 | dot (1 cell), bar of 2 |
| 2 | 3 clears | 5x5 | + bar of 3 |
| 3 | 8 clears | 6x6 | + 2x2 square, small corner of 3 (in all 4 turns) |
| 4 | 15 clears | 7x7 | + bar of 4 |
| 5 | 24 clears | 8x8 | + 2x3 rectangle (both ways round) |
| 6 (cap) | 35 clears | 9x9 | + bar of 5 |

Rationale: stage 1 is only dots and short bars, so a row of 5 can be filled in three to five drops and the first sparkle comes quickly. Each step adds one idea: a bit more room, or one new block, never a jump. The board grows one size at a time, and the number of clears needed grows (3, 5, 7, 9, 11 between steps) because longer lines take more drops to fill, so the pace of "a sparkle every few drops" stays about the same. **9x9 is the cap** (founder decision). Blocks top out at 5 cells (bar of 5) and 6 cells (2x3): all still simple rectangles and lines; no irregular shapes.

**Is 9x9 too much for a 4-year-old?** Deliberately not: it is the gentlest stage, not the hardest. Nothing falls, there is no clock, and a bigger board is more room, so fits are easier and clear-outs rarer; all 81 cells are never needed at once. Reaching it takes 35 self-made line clears (a long session; many sessions will end at 6x6 or 7x7, which is fine). The risks are visual: 36dp cells and a busier board. They are handled by story 40 (tray slots stay 72dp+, cells are not targets), story 41 (the block rides 64dp above the finger, snap reach never below ~40dp, a bolder ghost outline) and story 44 (a line-finishing block is offered on big boards). The block set works at 9x9 because a bar of 5 is only just over half a 9-cell line, so no single block clears a line by accident; and the corner and 2x3 give the tray shapes for tidying up around bars. If real play shows 9x9 is overwhelming for him, lowering the cap is a one-line change to the ramp.

**Out of scope for Milestone 5 (v2):** rotation, undo, hints, score/combos/stars/high scores (never: standing rule), boards larger than 9x9, blocks larger than 6 cells or irregular shapes (T, S, Z), themes or skins, saved progress, a "reset board" button, and any sound/music setting.

### Milestone 6 — Sixth game on the shelf: Paw Pop
*A toddler-friendly take on Space Invaders: bubbles and balloons drift down, a little friendly ship slides side to side at the bottom, keeps shooting stars, and every pop is a sparkle. "Paw Pop" is a working name chosen by the team (game `id` `paw-pop`); renaming is a content-only change. Like the earlier games it is a pure addition per `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md`: a new package plus one `GameCatalog` line, no change to any other game or the hub shell, and no dependence on another game's code.*

**Problem it solves:** a 2-5 year old loves cause and effect ("I moved, it popped") and a game that plays itself a little so he cannot fail, yet shooters teach lives, damage, enemies and scores. This one has no enemy, no damage, no end and no number. **Founder decisions (settled):** (1) bubbles and balloons (and critters in bubbles) drift down; stars pop them with a sparkle; nothing is hurt or exploded; (2) a target that gets past floats away harmlessly, with no lives, damage or game over; (3) endless play with no waves; power-ups sit inside targets and are released when popped.

Vocabulary: a **target** is a bubble or balloon; a **carrier** is a target with a **gift** (power-up) visible inside; a **star** is a shot; the **ship** is the child's. "sh" = screen heights (a 360 x 800dp phone: 1 sh = 800dp). Sizes below are for that reference screen.
